# The Fructus terminal

An ANSI terminal on the Fructus VGA display: 80 columns by 28 rows of 8×17
cells, in 640×480, fed from the UART at 115200 baud. Close enough to xterm
for vim, less and the shells with `TERM=xterm-256color`.

The source comments are the full description; this page is the overview.

## Running it

```
tools/fcc -Os -T ld/fructus-writethru.ld -Iterminal terminal/main.cc \
  terminal/terminal.cc terminal/decompose.cc terminal/font.cc \
  terminal/row.cc terminal/bidi.cc terminal/vectors.s -o build/terminal
node terminal/demo.mjs > build/demo.in
node tools/fcc-run.mjs build/terminal.bin --input build/demo.in --paced --x11 --scale 2
```

`node terminal/check.mjs` runs the cases on the host and on Fructus. It also
plays three inputs into `main.cc` at 115200 baud, and checks that no byte is
lost and that the last frame matches `preview.cc`'s, pixel for pixel.

## The pieces

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `terminal.h`, `terminal.cc` | bytes in, cells out: the escape sequences, the screen's state |
| `font.h`, `font.cc` | the font, cut into zones; `font.cc` is generated by `gen-font.mjs` from X11's 9x15 |
| `decompose.cc` | precomposed letters as a base and one mark; generated by `gen-decompose.mjs` |
| `bidi.h`, `bidi.cc` | a row's cells in the order the display shows them |
| `row.h`, `row.cc` | a row of cells into the frame buffer's arrays |
| `main.cc` | the program: the UART's ring, two buffers, the line tables |
| `dump.cc`, `preview.cc`, `rules-check.cc` | for the checks: the screen as text, as a picture, and the C glyph rule against the generator's |
| `demo.mjs` | a screen of colours, accents, symbols and boxes |
| `coverage.mjs`, `coverage.cc`, `coverage-extra.json` | each language's letters through the terminal, from CLDR's exemplars and alphabets by hand: what it can't show |

## What it understands

| Kind | Supported |
|---|---|
| C0 | BS HT LF VT FF CR SO SI; CAN and SUB abandon a sequence |
| ESC | `7 8 D E H M c = >`, `( )` with `0` or `B`, `#8` |
| CSI | `@ A B C D E F G H I J K L M P S T X Z` `` ` `` `a b c d e f g h l m n r s u`, `! p`, `SP k` |
| Modes | 4 insert, 8 bidi; `?6` origin, `?7` autowrap, `?25` cursor, `?47 ?1047 ?1049` alternate screen, `?2500 ?2501` bidi |
| SGR | 0 1 4 7 8 22 24 27 28, 30–37 39 40–47 49 90–97 100–107, 38/48 with `5;n` or `2;r;g;b` or the colon forms, `4:0`–`4:5` |
| Answers | DA (`CSI c`, `CSI > c`), DSR (`CSI 5 n`, `CSI 6 n`) |
| Ignored | OSC, DCS, PM, APC (read to their end); 58 and 59, the underline's colour |

**Not shown:** italic, dim, blink, double width and double height. Bold is
the brighter colour.

**Colours:** 16 ANSI colours (the VGA's), xterm's 256, and 24-bit direct
colour, all brought down to the display's 8-bit pixels: red 0–5, green
0–6, blue 0–4.

**Wide characters** (CJK, emoji) take two cells, shown as � and a space, so
that the host and the terminal agree on the columns. Joiners, variation
selectors, tags, and combining marks outside U+0300–U+036F have zero width
and are dropped.

**Scrolling moves no cells.** The display reads each scan line from its
line-table pointer, so a scroll rewrites pointers and draws only the row
that came in.

## The font

Each cell is 8×17, cut into five zones, and each zone has its own 256
codes:

| Zone | Lines | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| EDGE | 0–2, 13–16 | accents over capitals, descenders, accents below, the underline |
| RISER | 3–5 | capitals and ascenders above the x-height, accents over small letters |
| X_TOP | 6–7 | the top of the x-height |
| X_MIDDLE | 8–9 | |
| X_BOTTOM | 10–12 | down to the baseline |

A zone needs one code for each different slice, not one for each glyph, so
letters that share tops or bottoms share codes. Codes in use:

| | EDGE | RISER | X_TOP | X_MIDDLE | X_BOTTOM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Used, of 256 | 227 | 227 | 243 | 229 | 235 |

A cell holds a base and a mark. The mark is one combining accent
(U+0300–U+036F), or one of three special values: TONOS, SECOND or THIRD.
SECOND and THIRD choose the second and third sets of bases. That gives three
sets:

| Set | Size | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| First | 253 of 256 | ASCII; Latin-1's symbols and its letters of their own (Æ Ð Ø Þ ß æ ð ø þ); Latin Extended-A's (Đ Ħ Ĳ Ł Ŋ Œ Ŧ ...); quotes, dashes, €, arrows, ≤ ≥ ≠ π; box drawing (light, heavy, double, rounded); blocks, shades, quadrants, eighths |
| Second | 251 of 256 | symbols (✓ ✗ ■ □ ▲ ▼ ► ◄ ○ ⇐ ⇒ ⇔ ™ † ‡ ‰ ′ ″ №), maths (∞ √ ≈ ≡ ∑ ∂ ∫ ∀ ∃ ∈ ∅ ∧ ∨ ∩ ∪ ⌈⌉ ⌊⌋ and the rest of Markus Kuhn's SECS), ⁿ ⁰ ₀–₃, ℕ ℤ ℝ ℚ ℂ, Claude Code's symbols, ĸ Ə ə ƒ, Greek and Cyrillic letters that look like no Latin letter, Skolt Sámi's Ʒ ʒ Ǯ ǯ Ǥ ǥ |
| Third | 36 of 256 | Hebrew: the 22 letters, the 5 final forms, ׳ ״ ־ (the maqaf, redrawn at the top of the letters) and ₪; then letters that take no accent: Catalan's ŀ Ŀ, the long ſ, Mohawk's ʌ Ʌ |

**Accented letters** are a base and a mark, from 405 decompositions:

- **Latin:** all of Latin-1 Supplement and Latin Extended-A, plus the letters
  from Latin Extended-B and Latin Extended Additional that are a Latin-1 base
  with one of the same accents. The marks below the letter are the cedilla,
  the ogonek, and the comma, dot and macron below: Romanian's ș ț, Latvian's
  ģ ķ ļ ņ, Asturian's ḥ ḷ, Sicilian's ḍ, Mapudungun's ḻ ṉ ṯ.
- **i and j:** i is always a dotless ı with a dot, so an accent replaces the
  dot. j does the same with ȷ.
- **Overrides:** 23 of the 322 accented letters are letters that have ink
  where their accent goes, so they get codes of their own.

**Greek and Cyrillic** cover modern Greek, Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian,
Serbian, Macedonian and Bulgarian:

- Letters that look like Latin letters (Α, а, і, Ё ...) are drawn as the
  Latin letter, with an accent where they have one. So are a few of
  Cyrillic's extensions: һ as h, Ү as Y, the palochka Ӏ ӏ as I, ӕ Ӕ as æ Æ,
  ҫ Ҫ as ç Ç, and ә Ә as ə Ə. That covers Chechen, Kabardian, Adyghe and
  Ossetian.
- A Greek capital's tonos (Ά Έ Ή ...) is a mark of its own, placed at the
  letter's left.

**Aliases** draw 34 other code points as an existing base: ʼ as ’, − as -,
Hawaiian's ʻokina as ‘, the typographic spaces as a space, Claude Code's
spinner stars as *, and so on.

### Languages

Checked against the exemplar letters of CLDR 49's locales, and by hand for
some that CLDR lacks: `node terminal/coverage.mjs` with
[cldr-json](https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr-json) checked out in
`~/github`.

- **Europe:** the alphabet of every national language, in Latin, Greek and
  Cyrillic script. Also Asturian, Breton, Catalan, Cornish, Corsican, Faroese,
  Frisian, Friulian, Galician, Irish and Scottish Gaelic, Karelian,
  Kashubian, Komi, Latgalian, Latin, Ligurian, Low German, Luxembourgish,
  Manx, Occitan, Romansh, Rusyn, the Sámi languages (Northern, Inari, Skolt,
  Lule, Southern), Sardinian, Sicilian, Silesian, Sorbian, Venetian,
  Walloon and Welsh; and in the Caucasus, Chechen, Kabardian, Adyghe and
  Ossetian.
- **The Americas:** English, Spanish, Portuguese and French, and in Latin
  script Aymara, Greenlandic, Guaraní, Haitian, Hawaiian, Inuktitut, K'iche',
  Kaingang, Lakota, Mapudungun, Mohawk, Nahuatl, Nheengatu, Ojibwe,
  Papiamentu, Quechua and Yupik.

**A letter takes one accent.** A letter with two loses the second:

- Navajo's high-toned nasal vowels ą́ ę́ į́ ǫ́.
- Lithuanian's accented ą̃ ė́ ų́ ... (dictionaries only).
- Stress marks on Cyrillic letters from the second set, such as и́ ы́ э́ ю́
  я́ є́ (dictionaries and learners' texts). These letters take no accent at
  all, so even one is dropped.

**Missing:**
- Cyrillic extensions with no Latin lookalike: Tatar's җ ң ө ү, Bashkir's ғ
  ҙ ҡ ң ө ү, Chuvash's Ӳ, Mari's ҥ Ӱ, Udmurt's ӝ ӟ ӥ ӵ, Yakut's ҕ ҥ ө ү, and
  the capital Һ. They show as �.
- Yiddish's ligatures ײ װ ױ, and its points, which are dropped.
- Polytonic Greek.
- Armenian, Georgian, Cherokee and Canadian syllabics.
- Vietnamese's stacked accents, ỵ's dot (there is no room under the
  descender), and any letter with two marks that Unicode encodes
  precomposed (ǖ ...), which shows as �.
- Arabic, and every script further east.
- Hebrew points and cantillation, which are dropped.
- Hebrew presentation forms (U+FB1D–U+FB4F), which show as �.

**Room left:** 3 bases in the first set, 5 in the second, and 220 in the
third. X_TOP has 13 codes left and is the zone that fills first. Splitting a
zone costs 2,240 bytes of frame buffer, 80 bytes for each of the 28 rows.

## The frame buffer

Each of the two 32 KB buffers, at the CPU's WRITETHRU addresses:

| Address | Contents | Bytes |
|---|---|---|
| 0x8000 | the font, 17 lines × 256 codes | 4,352 |
| 0x9100 | codes, 5 zones × 28 rows × 80 | 11,200 |
| 0xbcc0 | foreground colours | 2,240 |
| 0xc580 | background colours | 2,240 |
| 0xce40 | a row of black, for the lines outside the text | 80 |
| 0xce90 | free | 8,560 |
| 0xf000 | line tables | 4,096 |

## Right to left

This follows Egmont Koblinger's
[BiDi in Terminal Emulators](https://terminal-wg.pages.freedesktop.org/bidi/),
at level 1. The cells stay in the order they arrived, so cursor addressing,
insert and delete work unchanged; `bidi.cc` reorders each row only when it
is drawn.

| Sequence | Effect |
|---|---|
| `CSI 8 h` / `CSI 8 l` | implicit (the default) / explicit |
| `CSI 2 SP k` | right to left |
| `CSI 1 SP k`, `CSI 0 SP k` | left to right |
| `CSI ? 2501 h` / `l` | autodetection: the direction from the first strong letter |
| `CSI ? 2500 h` / `l` | box drawing mirrored in right-to-left runs |

Each row stores its four properties in a byte, `Row::bidi`. The terminal
keeps a current set:
- A sequence received with the cursor at the start of a row also changes
  that row.
- A row takes the whole current set when a line feed (LF, VT, FF, IND or
  NEL) lands on it, and when it comes in blank (a scroll, IL or DL, an erase
  of the display, the alternate screen).
- An autowrapped row takes the properties of the row it wrapped from.

**Implicit mode** is the Unicode bidi algorithm (UAX #9) for the classes our
bases can have: L, R, EN, ES, ET, CS, ON and WS. `gen-font.mjs` gives every
base its class. `bidi.cc` applies W4–W7, N1–N2, I1–I2, L1, L2 and L4, so:
- Numbers read left to right, and ₪ beside a number stays with it.
- Spaces at the end of a row go to the paragraph's end, the left edge of a
  right-to-left row.
- Brackets and the other mirrored pairs in right-to-left runs show as their
  pair.

**Explicit mode** shows the cells as they are. A right-to-left row is
filled from the right edge, with mirrored characters mirrored.

**Speed:** a row with no right-to-left letter in a left-to-right paragraph
skips all of this. A right-to-left row costs about 18,000 cycles in
`bidi_order` and 38,000 to draw, about twice a left-to-right row's. A pass
that redraws all 28 rows takes about 1.5M cycles with the wait for vertical
sync. The 1,024-byte ring covers 2.2M.

### What is missing

- **Paragraphs are rows.** Koblinger's paragraphs run on across
  soft-wrapped rows. Here each row is reordered by itself, which keeps a
  row's drawing dependent on that row alone. Wrapped text that mixes
  directions can be laid out differently from VTE's.
- **Only level 1.**
  - Embeddings, overrides and isolates (LRE, RLO, FSI and the rest) are
    dropped as zero width.
  - The LRM and RLM marks are dropped too, so they can't steer how a neutral
    character is resolved.
- **No Arabic:** no AL or AN classes, no shaping, no presentation forms.
  The font has no Arabic anyway.
- **Mirroring needs a pair in the font.** The 17 pairs are ( ) < > [ ] { }
  « » ‹ › ≤ ≥ ≪ ≫ ⌈ ⌉ ⌊ ⌋ ⊂ ⊃ ⊆ ⊇ ⊄ ⊅ ⊈ ⊉ ⊊ ⊋ ∈ ∋ ∉ ∌. Mirrored
  characters with no pair, such as ∑ √ ∫ ∂ ∀ ∃ ≈, would need flipped glyphs,
  and are drawn as they are.
- **Box mirroring covers U+2500–U+257F pairs only.** Blocks (▌ ▐ ▖ ▗ ...)
  stay as they are, as the spec suggests.
- **No arrow-key swapping, mode 1243.** The terminal has no keyboard input.
- **SCP's second parameter:** values 1 and 2, which move the cursor, are
  ignored, as the spec allows. The SPD aliases (`CSI 0 SP S`, `CSI 3 SP S`)
  aren't recognized.
- **Erased cells are spaces.** The spec treats erased cells as distinct from
  spaces and ignores them inside a row. Here a blank in the middle of a row
  is a space, a neutral. At the end of a row the two behave the same.
- **ED** gives the current properties only to rows it erases whole, not to
  the cursor's row when it's partly erased.
- **Soft reset (`CSI ! p`)** leaves the current bidi properties as they are.
  RIS (`ESC c`) clears them.
- **No reporting:** DECRQM can't ask about modes 8, 2500 or 2501.
- **The cursor** is a block on its cell, wherever that cell is shown. There
  is no I-beam on the side of the cell that shows the character's direction.
- **A wide character** in a right-to-left run is reordered as two cells, �
  and a space.
