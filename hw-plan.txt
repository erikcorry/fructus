Here's an attempt to classify the instructions in terms of how essential they are and whether they would be part of an initial very minimalist implementation:

Stage 0 (essential - code gen will make heavy use of r5 temp register)

add/rsb/xor/and/or/lsr reg-reg-reg
mov reg, imm16
br with reg-reg and 6 conditions other than overflow
jmp #addr16
call #addr16
ret
ld reg reg #imm3 (only #0 supported)
st reg reg #imm3 (only #0 supported)

Stage 1 (very important)

shl: Can be emulated with repeated add.
asr: Can be emulated with lsr and some boolean bitops

Stage 2 (basic operations)

push: Can be emulated with add -2 and store
pop: Can be emulated with load and add 2

Stage 3 (immediate operands)

ALU mode reg-reg-imm3
Addressing mode reg-imm5
Support immediate non-zero offsets on ld and st
br with immediate operands

Stage 4 (PIC code)

callr
jmpr
Get PC in LR with callr #0

Stage 4 (8 bit operations)

br8
ld8
st8
pop8
push8

Stage 5 (system)

Interrupts & rti
Write protect bits per page
halt insn

Stage 6 (bit operations)

brclear
brset
#immbit5 addressing for ALU

Stage 7 (big immediates)

reg, reg, #imm10 addressing mode

Stage 8 (code size optimization)

push reg reg
push reg reg reg
pop reg reg
pop reg reg reg
one-byte forms of r0++, r0--, etc.
nop (previously emulated with or r0 r0 #0)
halt

Stage 9 (specialized instructions)

Bitrev
popcount
clz
sxt8
zxt8
overflow conditions for br and br8

Immediates: A 16 bit hidden reg, immreg, has the last two bytes from the insn
stream as a little endian word.

regport0 and regport1 can deliver any register to either side of the ALU.
They each have an address port that picks the register

regport0 address is one of:

regport1 address is one of:
  Low 3 bits of ucode rhs sources.     ; For single-byte instructions.
  immreg[9:8]:[immreg[0]]              ; For reg, reg, reg

ALU ports: lhs rhs op

lhs sources:  5 bits from the ucode determine where the ALU gets its lhs.
  Top 2 bits mux:
    00 regport0
    01 regport0
    10 regport0
    11 special register selector
  Same 2 bits go to the mux in front of regport0's address input:
    00 immreg[12:10])               ; For reg, reg, reg
                                    ;     reg, reg, imm3 (including load)
                                    ;     reg, reg, op (unary ops)
    01 immreg[15:13])               ; For reg, imm5 (including load)
                                    ;     conditional branches
    10 ucode 3 bits                 ; 3 bits from the ucode to pick a register
  Low 3 bits are copied to:
    regport0 register address mux

rhs sources:
 5 bits from the ucode determine where the ALU gets its rhs.
   Top 2 bits:
     00 regport1 output (low bit picks regport address mux)
     01 regport1 output (low bit picks regport address mux)
     10 immediate
     11 ucode
   Low 3 bits are copied to:
     The regport1 register selector mux, ucode input
     The immreg shifter:
       000 immreg                                        ; For mov #imm16, jump, jumpr etc.
       001 immreg[9:2]:immreg[1:0] sign extended to 16   ; For reg reg #imm10 mode
       010 immreg[4:0] sign extended to 16               ; For reg #imm5 mode
       011 imm3lut(immreg[9:8]:immreg[0])                ; For reg reg #imm3 mode, also for unary op
       100 immbitlut(immreg[3:0])                        ; For brclear, brset, #immbit5
       ; Here we could add "for free" other ways to treat the immreg, eg
       : - instructions with 8 bit immediates and implicit register.
       ; - Perhaps imm3 with no lut for a less hacky unary op selector
     Sign extended and used for the 11 ucode input.

ALU op: 4 lines from the ucode pick the ALU op
  add
  rsb
  xor
  and
  or
  shl
  shr
  asr
  unary


Destination:
  1 line determines whether a register from the file gets the ALU output written.
  3 lines select the register
  1 line says write to the PC
